## Primary result

Outcome: each item's error rate among the trained students of its study at update 40 (E1: confirmatory
v1's 40 runs; E2: confirmatory DC's 72 runs). Statistic: mean over the 20 set × skill cells of the
Spearman correlation between the difficulty estimate and the error rate.

| estimate | mean within-skill Spearman |
|---|---|
| **8-small-model panel** | **0.442** |
| Claude Opus 5.5 rating | 0.325 |

**H1: small panel − Opus = +0.118, 95% CI [+0.069, +0.167] (10,000 item bootstraps within cells; no
replicate ≤ 0). Supported.**

## Exploratory

Per cell, the small panel's correlation is higher in 15 of 20 (E1 9/10, E2 6/10). The rating does better
on form_structure_sense (both sets), and on E2's cross_text, central_ideas and command_of_evidence. The
largest gaps are boundaries, where the rating's correlation is negative (E1 −0.35, E2 −0.18) and the
panel's is near zero, rhetorical_synthesis on E1 (0.42 vs 0.19) and inferences on E2 (0.59 vs 0.16).

## Conclusion

On 1,199 held-out items, which neither estimator's makers had seen with student outcomes, **difficulty
calibrated from a panel of eight small open models predicts which items trained LLM students get wrong
clearly better than Claude Opus 5.5's direct judgement** (mean within-skill correlation 0.44 vs 0.32).
The result holds on each held-out set separately, for the base model before training, and for the
extended 15-member panel. Taken with confirmatory DC (FINDINGS #28), the panel is the better *measure*
of difficulty for LLM students; for *choosing training items* under the targeting rule tested, that
better measure did not translate into more learning.

Limits: one student model (OLMo-3-7B) and its GRPO-trained versions; one rater prompt (the rater was
asked about human SAT takers, the panel answers as LLMs); E2 was generated by the same model as the rater.
